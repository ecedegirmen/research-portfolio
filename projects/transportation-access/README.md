## Fellowship context and intended contribution

The Carolina Impact Fellowship supports students in creating research, tools, and other resources for communities across North Carolina. Through my project, I aim to make transportation barriers to education and training easier to identify and explain. The planned dashboard and report are intended to help community colleges, workforce organizations, and transportation partners consider where access could be improved.

## What I'm investigating

An education or training program can be nearby and still be difficult to reach. I'm developing a project on transportation barriers for young adults in North Carolina, asking whether available transportation allows someone to arrive for a class and return home afterward.

I'm considering Orange County or Durham County as the initial study area. The final geography, training sites, and course selection are not yet fixed. This is a separate project from my Opportunity Scholarship research.

## Proposed evidence

| Information | What it would contribute | Documentation needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Training-site locations | Establish destinations | Address, coordinates, geocoding method, and verification |
| Course schedules | Define required arrival and departure times | Term, days, start/end times, and date checked |
| Transit routes and schedules | Assess connections that operate at relevant times | Schedule version, service days, transfers, and service limits |
| Census/ACS household vehicle availability | Describe transportation context | Table, release, geography, denominator, and uncertainty |
| Published service requirements | Identify constraints beyond route proximity | Reservation rules, eligibility, and operating hours where relevant |

## Planned workflow

1. **Set the scope.** Select the county, population, programs, and a manageable set of class schedules.
2. **Create a source register.** Record where each location, schedule, and rule came from and when it was checked.
3. **Define access.** Choose explicit assumptions for arrival time, walking, transfers, waiting, and the return journey.
4. **Prepare the data.** Standardize addresses, coordinates, schedule fields, and geographic identifiers.
5. **Assess feasible journeys.** Evaluate travel at the actual class times rather than treating distance to a stop as sufficient.
6. **Test sensitivity.** Compare results under alternative time and transfer assumptions.
7. **Communicate the findings.** Present accessible maps or charts, explain exclusions, and separate measured barriers from proposed responses.

## Limits I need to address

Household vehicle availability does not tell me which individuals can use a vehicle at a particular time. Published transit schedules describe planned service, and may not capture reliability or every practical barrier. Any accessibility measure will depend on the origins and travel assumptions selected.

## Intended outputs

- An interactive dashboard explaining patterns and assumptions.
- A research report with practical recommendations.
- A reproducible analysis repository with source notes and documented processing steps.

**Published findings:** None yet. The next milestone is to choose the initial geography and verify a small sample of training sites, class times, and transit schedules before expanding the analysis.
