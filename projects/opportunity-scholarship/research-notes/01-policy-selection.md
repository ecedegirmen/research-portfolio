## Policy and institutional setting

The North Carolina General Assembly expanded Opportunity Scholarship eligibility through House Bill 259, enacted as S.L. 2023-134, the 2023 Appropriations Act. The legislation became law on October 3, 2023, and the eligibility changes applied beginning with the 2024–25 school year.

The North Carolina State Education Assistance Authority (NCSEAA) administers the program.

## What changed

Beginning in 2024–25, the policy removed the previous household-income cap and prior-public-school enrollment requirement. These changes expanded the pool of students eligible for scholarships toward tuition and fees at eligible nonpublic schools.

Household income continued to determine scholarship amounts rather than eligibility. The initial policy-selection write-up identified approximate 2024–25 awards of $3,000–$7,000 per student; exact award tiers and amounts will be recorded from the relevant administrative release before quantitative analysis.

## Population covered

The initial policy review identified North Carolina students who reside in the state, are eligible to attend a North Carolina public school, have not received a high school diploma, are not enrolled full-time in a postsecondary institution taking at least 12 credit hours, and have not been placed in a nonpublic school or facility by a public agency at public expense.

Removing the income cap and prior-public-school enrollment requirement made the program accessible to a broader range of households, including those with students already attending private schools.

## Economic motivation

State-funded private-school tuition subsidies lower the effective price of private schooling for recipient households. Expanding eligibility could therefore change household school choice, private-school demand, and enrollment patterns across public and private schools.

These mechanisms motivate research on educational access, the distribution of benefits, and implications for public spending. They are hypotheses and research questions, not established effects of the expansion.
