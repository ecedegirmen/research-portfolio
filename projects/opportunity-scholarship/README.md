# North Carolina Opportunity Scholarship expansion

[Back to portfolio](../../README.md)

**Context:** ECON 440, Analysis of Public Finance, UNC Chapel Hill  
**Status:** Research in development; data assessment and empirical design  
**Collaboration:** Two-person course project. I initiated the topic and wrote the first policy-selection draft.

## What I'm investigating

I'm studying North Carolina's 2024–25 Opportunity Scholarship expansion, with a focus on educational access, school enrollment, and implications for public spending. I want to understand who the expansion reaches and what the available evidence can tell us about changes in enrollment and funding.

A central distinction is between describing changes after a policy and establishing that the policy caused them. The research design is still being developed.

## Data under assessment

These are candidate sources for the project, not a claim that every dataset has already been downloaded, cleaned, or merged.

| Source | Intended use | Questions to resolve before analysis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NCSEAA scholarship reports | Document participation, awards, and policy timing | Which years and geographic breakdowns are comparable? |
| NC public-school enrollment records | Describe public enrollment over time | Which enrollment measure and reporting date should be used consistently? |
| NC private-school records | Describe private-school enrollment | How consistent are school coverage and reporting across years? |
| ACS school-enrollment and household-income tables | Provide demographic and economic context | Which geography, population, and estimate period match the question? |

A source register and variable dictionary will be added after the exact files and releases are selected.

## Planned workflow

1. **Define the policy and outcomes.** Document the eligibility change, implementation timeline, population of interest, and outcomes before choosing a model.
2. **Assess data feasibility.** Inventory years, geographic units, reporting definitions, missingness, and access conditions.
3. **Build comparable measures.** Preserve original files; use scripts to clean identifiers and dates, reconcile units, and record exclusions.
4. **Describe the evidence.** Examine enrollment, scholarship participation, and spending patterns, with denominators and source notes.
5. **Evaluate an empirical design.** Consider whether a defensible comparison group and sufficient pre-policy observations exist. Difference-in-differences or an event study would require an explicit identification argument; neither is yet an implemented result.
6. **Check alternative explanations.** Consider demographic changes, enrollment trends, other policy changes, and differences in data coverage.
7. **Write the interpretation.** Distinguish descriptive evidence, estimated relationships, and any supported causal conclusions.

## Limits I need to address

- An increase in private-school enrollment does not by itself identify students who transferred from public schools.
- Scholarship recipients may include students already attending private schools.
- Geographic aggregates cannot establish individual household behavior.
- Overlapping ACS multiyear estimates need careful interpretation when comparing periods.
- A fiscal analysis needs explicit assumptions about costs and funding responses.

## Intended outputs

A research paper, documented analysis code, source and variable documentation, and figures with clear interpretation. I hope to develop the work further through independent undergraduate research and a symposium presentation.

**Published findings:** None yet. The next milestone is a verified data inventory and a feasible, precisely defined research question.
